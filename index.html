Have you ever felt the thrill of stepping into a stadium for the very first time—the lights, the crowd, the cheers echoing all around you?
For many children, such a moment can feel out of reach. But thanks to your generosity, on July 22, a group of students experienced that joy for the very first time.

At Teach For Taiwan, we support over 120 teachers serving in rural communities every year. With your help, we also open doors to experiences beyond the classroom—moments that inspire courage, resilience, and hope.

This summer, through a special partnership with the TSG Hawks baseball team, children not only attended a professional game but also interacted with the players themselves. It was more than just a game—it was a lesson in perseverance, teamwork, and dreaming big.

Anticipation Beyond the Classroom

What does excitement look like when it can't be contained?
Before the event, the students could hardly wait. Their teacher, TFT 9th Cohort alumnus Hung-Yu Lin, shared how even during math lessons, the children calculated player stats, and in their assignments they wrote about their hopes of seeing the game in person.
One student told us: "When you face setbacks in a game, don't give up—because never giving up is true victory."

A First Step Into the Ballpark

For most of the children, this was their very first time entering such a grand stadium. Wide-eyed with curiosity, they nervously asked the players questions during the Q&A session, their voices filled with both excitement and awe.
When the game began, the energy of the crowd swept them away. Some waved cheering sticks in rhythm with the chants; others proudly raised handmade posters to shout encouragement for the Hawks. And when their long-awaited idols finally stepped onto the field, their joy could not be hidden.

Lessons That Last Beyond the Game

On the way home, the children couldn't stop talking about what they had experienced:

"The most unforgettable part was watching Mo Ying overcome sadness and still contribute to the team."
"This was my first time seeing such an amazing game in a real stadium. I hope one day we can play baseball too."
The cheers and the atmosphere lit a spark in their hearts. For some, it was the birth of a new dream—to one day step onto the field themselves.

More Than a Game—A Lesson in Life

Thanks to your support, this event became so much more than just a day at the ballpark. It became a once-in-a-lifetime learning experience outside the classroom—one filled with courage, teamwork, and inspiration.
Your generosity gave children not only the chance to watch their heroes play, but also the belief that they, too, can overcome challenges and chase their dreams.

From the bottom of our hearts, thank you. Together, we are ensuring that every child—no matter where they come from—can discover the joy of learning, both inside and beyond the classroom.
If you'd like to see how learning continues beyond the classroom, we invite you to stay connected—through our website, Instagram, and Facebook, where stories of courage, opportunity, and care continue to unfold.

Have you ever wondered how many times a child can start over?
How many "first days" can one hold before they begin to feel ordinary—or exhausting?

Another First Day—Again

In my first year of teaching, my class had 19 students. By the second year, transfer students kept arriving—one after another. The classroom grew more crowded, lessons were interrupted more often, and someone joked, "You must be a great teacher—everyone keeps transferring here!"

But I couldn't laugh. What I saw were the quiet, heavy stories behind each transfer.

Children Who Are Always Starting Over

These children reminded me of seedlings blown by the wind. Just as they begin to take root, they are uprooted again—forced to start over in a new place.

Behind every transfer is a lack of choice: changes in caregivers, learning struggles, parents moving for work.

Changing What Children Arrive To

We may not be able to stop children from moving again and again. But we can change what awaits them when they arrive.

Sometimes, education begins with something simple: open arms, a sincere smile, and the words— "You're welcome here."

Thank You for Making This Possible

Your support makes stories like this possible. Every donation helps ensure these changes are not one-time moments, but lasting ones.

Today, we'd like to share something a little different. This is not a report or a summary—it is a letter. A letter written by Jia-Sheng Lee, a TFT alumna who served in rural Pingtung, to the graduating class she walked with for two years.

Dear My Children,

Congratulations on your graduation. This is a letter I've wanted to write for a long time.
You may not remember every word, but these lines carry my truest feelings—my graduation gift to you.

Two years ago, I became a homeroom teacher for the first time. I was nervous and inexperienced, stepping into the classroom with equal parts excitement and fear. You were my very first class—and the beginning of my teaching journey.

In those early days, every morning felt like opening a mystery box. Some days were joyful, others heavy. There were conflicts, tears, and moments when I doubted myself so deeply that I was afraid to walk into the classroom. Once, after a fight broke out, I cried in the room—then had to pull myself together and keep going.
That was when I realized: you had already found a place in my heart.

Growing, Quietly and Steadily

Over time, we changed—together. From fifth grade to sixth, you learned to remind one another, to take responsibility, to calm yourselves, and to care for the group. These changes may have felt small to you, but I saw them clearly—and I will never forget them.

We planned dreams and made them happen. We traveled together, laughed endlessly, and created a graduation book filled with our shared memories. Other teachers began to notice your kindness and growth. You really did grow up.

Your emotions rose and fell, and sometimes confused you. But remember—these feelings are proof that you are growing. They will not last forever. One day, you will feel steadier, stronger, and more like yourself.

What I Hope You Carry Forward

More than anything, I hope you grow into confident people. Not the strongest—but those who know their own uniqueness and where they shine.

Wherever you go next, remember this: You have tried, fallen, and stood back up. You have cared for friends—and been cared for in return.

If one day you feel tired or lost, remember—you can always come back. The place that grew with you is still here. And so is the teacher who once walked beside you.

You will become even more capable than you are now. Every small effort you make will one day become a treasure no one can take away.

Thank you for walking with me through the very first chapter of my teaching life. I will always remember you—my gentle, funny, hardworking children.
Happy graduation. I will always care for you.
—Teacher Jia-Sheng
